# Further reading

This chapter collects the sources behind each chapter of the book and the resources we recommend for going deeper. Within each chapter, the first entry marked **Start here** is the best next step for most readers; the others go deeper or cover specific techniques. Entries marked *(free)* can be read or watched without a subscription.

## Textbooks

- **Murphy, D.B. & Davidson, M.W. (2012).** *Fundamentals of Light Microscopy and Electronic Imaging*, 2nd ed. Wiley-Blackwell. ISBN 978-0-471-69214-0.\
  **Start here.** The closest match to this book in scope and level, from optics and contrast techniques to confocal, two-photon, super-resolution and digital imaging.
- **Mertz, J. (2019).** *Introduction to Optical Microscopy*, 2nd ed. Cambridge University Press. [doi:10.1017/9781108552660](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108552660)\
  The rigorous version of this book: Fourier optics, coherence, 3D imaging theory, noise and almost every technique covered here, with problems at the end of each chapter.
- **Pawley, J.B. (ed.) (2006).** *Handbook of Biological Confocal Microscopy*, 3rd ed. Springer. [doi:10.1007/978-0-387-45524-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-45524-2)\
  The reference work for laser-scanning microscopy, with chapters on lasers, detectors, sampling, aberrations, photobleaching, light-sheet and nonlinear microscopy.
- **Hecht, E. (2017).** *Optics*, 5th ed. Pearson. ISBN 978-0-13-397722-6.\
  The standard undergraduate optics textbook, for anything about waves, rays, interference, diffraction and Fourier optics that this book treats briefly.
- **Goodman, J.W. (2017).** *Introduction to Fourier Optics*, 4th ed. W. H. Freeman. ISBN 978-1-319-11916-4.\
  The classic text on diffraction, lenses as Fourier transformers and transfer functions.
- **Lakowicz, J.R. (2006).** *Principles of Fluorescence Spectroscopy*, 3rd ed. Springer. [doi:10.1007/978-0-387-46312-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-46312-4)\
  The reference for fluorescence photophysics, including lifetimes, quenching, energy transfer, FLIM and FCS.

## Online courses and resources

- **iBiology Microscopy Series.** [ibiology.org/online-biology-courses/microscopy-series](https://www.ibiology.org/online-biology-courses/microscopy-series/) *(free)*\
  **Start here.** Lectures and lab demonstrations by leading microscopists, from basic optics and Köhler alignment to cameras and super-resolution.
- **Microcourses** (Jennifer Waters and colleagues, Harvard Medical School). [youtube.com/microcourses](https://www.youtube.com/microcourses) *(free)*\
  Short, precise videos on single concepts: Nyquist sampling, cameras, filters, objectives.
- **Microtutor.** [microtutorcourses.org](https://microtutorcourses.org) *(free)*\
  A self-paced, interactive light microscopy course from the same team.
- **Nikon MicroscopyU.** [microscopyu.com](https://www.microscopyu.com) *(free)*
- **ZEISS Microscopy Online Campus.** [zeiss-campus.magnet.fsu.edu](https://zeiss-campus.magnet.fsu.edu) *(free)*
- **Evident (Olympus) Microscopy Resource Center.** [evidentscientific.com/en/microscope-resource](https://evidentscientific.com/en/microscope-resource) *(free)*\
  Three manufacturer sites with hundreds of tutorials and interactive simulations. Some content is old, but the principles have not changed.
- **FocalPlane** (The Company of Biologists). [focalplane.biologists.com](https://focalplane.biologists.com) *(free)*\
  A community site for microscopists, with a curated list of learning resources.
- **FPbase.** [fpbase.org](https://www.fpbase.org) *(free)*\
  A searchable database of fluorescent proteins with a spectra viewer for checking filter and laser combinations.

## Chapter 1: What is light?

- **Wollman, A.J.M., Nudd, R., Hedlund, E.G. & Leake, M.C. (2015).** From Animaculum to single molecules: 300 years of the light microscope. *Open Biology* 5, 150019. [doi:10.1098/rsob.150019](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsob.150019) *(free)*\
  **Start here.** A readable open-access history from van Leeuwenhoek to single-molecule imaging and light-sheet microscopy.
- **Nature Milestones in Light Microscopy (2009).** Supplement to *Nature Cell Biology*, *Nature Methods* and *Nature Reviews Molecular Cell Biology*. [Milestone 1 (PDF)](https://www.nature.com/articles/milelight01.pdf) *(free)*\
  Short articles on the key developments from 1595 to 2006, each with its primary references. The original website is archived, but the individual Milestones are still online as PDFs with numbered addresses, for example [Milestone 3 on the Abbe limit](https://www.nature.com/articles/milelight03.pdf) and [Milestone 21 on super-resolution](https://www.nature.com/articles/milelight21.pdf).
- **Hecht, E. (2017).** *Optics*, 5th ed. Pearson.\
  Chapters 1 to 3 cover the history of optics, wave motion and the electromagnetic and photon descriptions of light.
- **Vale, R.** History of light microscopy, iBiology Short Microscopy Series. [ibiology.org](https://www.ibiology.org/online-biology-courses/short-microscopy-series/) *(free)*
- **Minsky, M. (1988).** Memoir on inventing the confocal scanning microscope. *Scanning* 10, 128–138. [doi:10.1002/sca.4950100403](https://doi.org/10.1002/sca.4950100403)\
  The inventor's own account of the 1955 microscope. Free text at [MIT](https://www.mit.edu/~dxh/marvin/web.media.mit.edu/~minsky/papers/ConfocalMemoir.html).
- **Amos, W.B. & White, J.G. (2003).** How the confocal laser scanning microscope entered biological research. *Biology of the Cell* 95, 335–342.
- **Betzig, E. (2015).** Nobel Lecture: Single molecules, cells, and super-resolution optics. *Reviews of Modern Physics* 87, 1153–1168. [doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.87.1153](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.87.1153)
- **Hell, S.W. (2015).** Nobel Lecture: Nanoscopy with freely propagating light. *Reviews of Modern Physics* 87, 1169–1181. [doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.87.1169](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.87.1169)
- **Moerner, W.E. (2015).** Nobel Lecture: Single-molecule spectroscopy, imaging, and photocontrol: Foundations for super-resolution microscopy. *Reviews of Modern Physics* 87, 1183.\
  The three 2014 Nobel lectures tell the story of super-resolution from the people who made it happen. All three are also free at [nobelprize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org).

## Chapter 2: Geometrical optics

- **Hecht, E. (2017).** *Optics*, 5th ed. Pearson.\
  **Start here.** Chapters 5 and 6 cover lenses, ray tracing and the Seidel aberrations in more depth than this book.
- **Murphy, D.B. & Davidson, M.W. (2012).** *Fundamentals of Light Microscopy and Electronic Imaging*, 2nd ed.\
  The chapter on lenses and geometrical optics connects aberrations directly to objective design and correction classes.
- **Pawley, J.B. (ed.) (2006).** *Handbook of Biological Confocal Microscopy*, 3rd ed.\
  The chapter on aberrations induced by refractive index mismatch quantifies what happens when the immersion medium and the sample do not match.

## Chapter 3: Widefield microscopes and image formation

- **Murphy, D.B. & Davidson, M.W. (2012).** *Fundamentals of Light Microscopy and Electronic Imaging*, 2nd ed.\
  **Start here.** The chapters on diffraction and spatial resolution, phase contrast and darkfield, and DIC are the best practical complement to this chapter.
- **iBiology Microscopy Series.** Köhler illumination alignment, demonstrated by Ron Vale. [ibiology.org](https://www.ibiology.org/online-biology-courses/microscopy-series/) *(free)*
- **Evident Microscopy Resource Center.** Köhler illumination interactive tutorials. [evidentscientific.com](https://evidentscientific.com/en/microscope-resource/knowledge-hub/anatomy/anatomy) *(free)*\
  Lets you practise aligning a virtual microscope.
- **Mertz, J. (2019).** *Introduction to Optical Microscopy*, 2nd ed.\
  Chapters 10 and 11 treat widefield and interference microscopy, including phase contrast and DIC, with full theory.
- **Zernike, F. (1942).** Phase contrast, a new method for the microscopic observation of transparent objects. Part I. *Physica* 9, 686–698. [doi:10.1016/S0031-8914(42)80035-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0031-8914(42)80035-X)
- **Zernike, F. (1955).** How I discovered phase contrast. *Science* 121, 345–349. [doi:10.1126/science.121.3141.345](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.121.3141.345)\
  Zernike's Nobel lecture: a short, readable account of how a defect in a diffraction grating led to phase contrast.
- **Abbe, E. (1873).** Beiträge zur Theorie des Mikroskops und der mikroskopischen Wahrnehmung. *Archiv für mikroskopische Anatomie* 9, 413–468. [doi:10.1007/BF02956173](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02956173)\
  For German readers only: the original paper on the resolution limit.

## Chapter 4: Fluorescence microscopy

- **Lichtman, J.W. & Conchello, J.-A. (2005).** Fluorescence microscopy. *Nature Methods* 2, 910–919. [doi:10.1038/nmeth817](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth817)\
  **Start here.** A clear review of excitation, emission and how a fluorescence microscope works.
- **Lakowicz, J.R. (2006).** *Principles of Fluorescence Spectroscopy*, 3rd ed. Springer.\
  The first chapters cover the Jablonski diagram, lifetimes, quantum yields and quenching in full.
- **Tsien, R.Y. (1998).** The green fluorescent protein. *Annual Review of Biochemistry* 67, 509–544.\
  The classic review on GFP: structure, chromophore formation and the first colour variants.
- **Shaner, N.C., Steinbach, P.A. & Tsien, R.Y. (2005).** A guide to choosing fluorescent proteins. *Nature Methods* 2, 905–909. [doi:10.1038/nmeth819](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth819)
- **Lambert, T.J. (2019).** FPbase: a community-editable fluorescent protein database. *Nature Methods* 16, 277–278. [doi:10.1038/s41592-019-0352-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0352-8)\
  Use FPbase itself for up-to-date choices; the 2005 guide explains the criteria.
- **Grimm, J.B. et al. (2015).** A general method to improve fluorophores for live-cell and single-molecule microscopy. *Nature Methods* 12, 244–250. [doi:10.1038/nmeth.3256](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.3256)\
  The Janelia Fluor dyes, and a good introduction to combining self-labelling tags with bright synthetic dyes.
- **Resch-Genger, U. et al. (2008).** Quantum dots versus organic dyes as fluorescent labels. *Nature Methods* 5, 763–775. [doi:10.1038/nmeth.1248](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.1248)
- **Laissue, P.P. et al. (2017).** Assessing phototoxicity in live fluorescence imaging. *Nature Methods* 14, 657–661. [doi:10.1038/nmeth.4344](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.4344)\
  Why all live fluorescence imaging causes some damage, and how to measure and report it.

## Chapter 6: A gentle introduction to Fourier optics

- **Sanderson, G. (3Blue1Brown) (2018).** But what is the Fourier Transform? A visual introduction. [3blue1brown.com/lessons/fourier-transforms](https://www.3blue1brown.com/lessons/fourier-transforms) *(free)*\
  **Start here.** The best visual intuition for what a Fourier transform does, before any optics.
- **Hecht, E. (2017).** *Optics*, 5th ed. Pearson.\
  Chapter 11 is a gentle, well-illustrated introduction to Fourier optics.
- **Murphy, D.B. & Davidson, M.W. (2012).** *Fundamentals of Light Microscopy and Electronic Imaging*, 2nd ed.\
  The chapter on diffraction and interference in image formation explains Abbe's theory with microscope examples.
- **Goodman, J.W. (2017).** *Introduction to Fourier Optics*, 4th ed. W. H. Freeman.\
  Chapter 6 derives the Fourier-transforming properties of lenses, including the object positions discussed in 6.1; chapter 7 covers coherent and incoherent transfer functions.
- **Mertz, J. (2019).** *Introduction to Optical Microscopy*, 2nd ed.\
  Chapters 2 to 5 build Fourier optics specifically for microscopy; Appendix A collects the Fourier transform properties.
