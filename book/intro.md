# Advanced Light Microscopy and Bioimaging

*Marco Locarno, Utrecht University*

Why can't an ordinary microscope show you a single protein, and how did a few physicists get around that anyway? This book follows light from the lamp to the camera and answers that question one piece at a time. You will see why a lens hands you the Fourier transform of your sample for free, how a pinhole or a thin sheet of light can slice through an embryo without cutting it, and how blinking molecules and doughnut-shaped beams brought resolution down to a few nanometres. Along the way you will learn to read a microscope schematic like an open book, and to choose, or build, the right instrument for your own biological question.

Most sections include worked examples and interactive simulations: use them! Changing a parameter and watching what happens is often the fastest way to understand a technique or trade-off. Corrections and suggestions are welcome through the "Open issue" button at the top of each page.
